Lead scoring model

Six installs a month to more than fifty

Field reps were working territory by instinct. I built a scoring model with Claude that ranked every business in a market on the things that actually predicted a close, then turned the output into routed lists reps could run.

Proximity to existing fiber mattered more than anything else, because it drove both install cost and speed to live. A large account 2,000 feet off the route was worth less than a smaller one sitting 200 feet away. Once the model weighted for that, the same reps in the same territory started closing an order of magnitude more.

What it changed

From a list to a route

Scoring alone does not move volume. The output had to become something a rep could execute against on a Tuesday morning, so I built optimized territory routes on top of it using KMZ mapping and Vetro. Reps got a sequence to work through. That is where the 700% came from.

I do not know what the role is yet

You asked what I would be open to and I said customer success, account management, program and operations. That spread is honest. My last six years have been a mixture of all three, and the roles where I did my best work were the ones that refused to sit in one lane.

How I work
01

I built my current role from scratch

Wire 3 knew they had a need. They could not tell me exactly what it was. Over two years I learned the business well enough to name the gaps myself, and the job I have now is the one I built by filling them. Nobody handed me a job description.

02

I direct teams that do not report to me

The SMB team, the community team, and the DSR managers all take direction from me and none of them work for me. Everything I get done runs on being useful to those teams, which is the only version of this job I have ever had.

03

I take the thing nobody owns

Most of what I have built started as a problem sitting between two teams with no name on it. I pick those up before anyone assigns them, which is how I ended up running the project queue for the whole company.

04

I sit on both sides of the build

I work directly with customers, communities, and vendors, and I work directly with our engineers and C-suite. Being fluent enough on both sides means what gets built is what the client actually needed. I am not technical. I am useful to technical people.

What I am after

Why I keep reaching out

I have gotten more out of the last two years than the rest of my career combined, and the reason is that almost nothing was built yet when I got there. I want more of that.

Fortune 500 does not move me. I have had those companies as clients and the pace is not something I want to live inside. What I am optimizing for now is proximity to people who know how to build and scale a business from nothing, close enough that some of how they do it transfers.

I have been paying attention to what you are building for a while. That is the actual reason I keep reaching out, and it is not something I do casually.

My background is eclectic and it has never fit cleanly into one seat. At a company of a dozen people that tends to be an asset. I want to wear as many hats as the stage calls for, move fast, build the plane while it is flying, and be worth considerably more than I cost.
